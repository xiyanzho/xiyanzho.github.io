Goal-Conditioned Contrastive Reinforcement Learning in Discrete Action Spaces
Research Assistant · advised by Kellen Kanarios · Jan 2026 – Present

Reimplementing contrastive RL in JAX and adapting it to discrete control, where the critic is learned by classification against in-batch negatives rather than by bootstrapped TD updates. I build grid-world benchmarks that each isolate one hypothesis about what the contrastive objective can represent. The central finding is that goal-conditioned critics stop discriminating between actions when a goal admits more than one satisfying state: success and a direct probe of the critic ("critic lift") fall with the number of satisfiers rather than with task difficulty. The effect replicates as a dose-response in a second environment, is isolated by a control that changes only the goal's form, and also appears in a matched Double-DQN baseline — so it is a property of goal-conditioned value learning, not of the contrastive objective alone. Getting there meant correcting ourselves: an earlier sharp collapse was an artifact of the InfoNCE direction, and re-running everything on the corrected configuration withdrew three claims while the underlying effect held. We are preparing a preprint.

A Diagnostic Study of Sparsity-Promoting ADMM for Multi-Obstacle Trajectory Optimization
Independent course research project · ECE 559: Optimization for Signal Processing & ML · Winter 2026

Formulated 2D double-integrator navigation with an ℓ2,1 group-sparsity penalty on control, so the robot coasts between brief actuation bursts, and solved it with a two-block ADMM splitting (dynamics QP, obstacle projection, block soft-thresholding) with over-relaxation and adaptive penalty scaling. The method delivers sparsity — about 60% zero-input timesteps at lower control effort than quadratic MPC — but diagnostics that score safety separately on the dynamically feasible trajectory and on the projected auxiliary one expose a structural failure: across 10 randomized environments the auxiliary trajectory is collision-free in 8/10 while the real one is collision-free in 0/10. Sweeps over λ and iteration budget show the gap does not close with more iterations, pointing to greedy non-convex projection rather than early stopping.

Replicating and Extending SimGRL: Robustness in Visual RL under Dynamic Scene Perturbations
Team course project · EECS 498: Machine Learning Research Experience · Fall 2025

Replicated SimGRL (NeurIPS 2024) on DMControl-GB Walker Walk under a 60K-step compute budget on the Great Lakes cluster: SimGRL beat the SVEA baseline in every evaluation mode, by 107% on Video Hard, matching the original paper's trends. The team then tested four augmentation extensions. My part was Hybrid-SRO, which adds per-frame photometric perturbations (color jitter, grayscale, blur, pixel shifts) to SimGRL's background overlays. I implemented and tuned it and ran its experiments. It did not beat SimGRL on average, but it cut the across-seed standard deviation from about 75 to about 24 and came within 2% of SimGRL on Video Hard.

Bayesian Optimization: Max-value Entropy Search vs. Expected Improvement
Course project · EECS 498: Bayesian Machine Learning · Winter 2026

Implemented Gaussian-process Bayesian optimization from scratch (NumPy/SciPy): marginal-likelihood hyperparameter fitting with BIC-based kernel selection, expected improvement, and a re-implementation of Max-value Entropy Search (Wang & Jegelka, ICML 2017). On hyperparameter-tuning benchmarks MES unexpectedly underperformed EI; the likely cause is a degenerate fitted GP lengthscale, which leaves MES little structure from which to estimate the maximum.
